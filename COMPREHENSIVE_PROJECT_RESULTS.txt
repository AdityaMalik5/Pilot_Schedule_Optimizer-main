================================================================================
  FLIGHT SCHEDULE OPTIMIZER (test4.py) — COMPREHENSIVE RESULTS & METHODOLOGY
================================================================================
Generated as a reference for reporting, review, or external analysis (e.g. upload
to another tool). Numeric KPIs that depend on YOUR uploaded CSV are not hard-
coded here; this file documents what the application computes, how, and which
values are fixed in code vs. data-driven.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE OF THE SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Streamlit application (test4.py) supports:
  • Loading and normalizing flight CSV data (routes, times, aircraft, delays).
  • Congestion and delay-pattern analytics for configured Indian hub airports.
  • Cascading delay / turnaround risk scoring per flight.
  • Interactive schedule tuning (simulated time shifts with before/after delay
    risk and downstream aircraft impact).
  • Enhanced ML models predicting arrival-delay binary outcomes using engineered
    features (including peak hours, weekends, turnaround pressure, cascading risk).
  • Pilot fatigue risk (Random Forest on synthetic rule-based training data) with
    deterministic rule checks; schedule optimization proposes crew swaps and small
    duty spacing shifts based on that model.
  • NLP query interface (when Sentence-BERT is available) over column semantics.

“Efficiency” in this project is expressed through: lower composite slot scores
(congestion + delay + cascading), lower historical delay rate at a slot, reduced
fatigue critical probability after swaps, and qualitative banding (EXCELLENT → AVOID).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA PIPELINE & KEY DERIVED METRICS (clean_flight_data_enhanced)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
After cleaning, each flight row includes:
  • DepDelay, ArrDelay (minutes; ATD−STD, ATA−STA).
  • DepHour, ArrHour (from actual times).
  • Turnaround_Time: gap in minutes between this flight’s ATA and the next
    scheduled departure on the same aircraft registration.
  • Is_Quick_Turnaround: 1 if Turnaround_Time < 90 minutes.
  • Cascading_Risk_Score (0–1 scale, sum of three weighted components):
      – 0.4 × I(DepDelay > 15 min)
      – 0.3 × Is_Quick_Turnaround
      – 0.3 × I(DepHour in peak set {7,8,9,17,18,19,20})
  • ArrDelayBinary / DepDelayBinary: 1 if delay > 15 minutes (used for ML target
    and feature engineering in enhanced models).

Distance is approximated as AirTime_minutes × 8 (heuristic).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. AIRPORT CONFIGURATION (fixed parameters — AIRPORT_CONFIG)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Six airports are modeled with declared hourly capacity, peak hours, and stress
multipliers. Congestion uses these together with observed traffic counts.

Code  Airport   Capacity/hr  Peak hours (examples)     weather_delays  ground_congestion
----  --------  -------------  ------------------------  --------------  -----------------
BOM   Mumbai    45             8–10, 17–20               0.15            0.85
DEL   Delhi     80             7–9, 18–20                0.18            0.90
BLR   Bangalore 50             8–9, 17–19                0.10            0.80
CCU   Kolkata   35             7–8, 17–18                0.14            0.75
IXC   Chandigarh 15            7–8, 16–17                0.10            0.60
BBI   Bhubaneswar 20           8–9, 18–19                0.12            0.70

(Runway counts are stored per airport for context; utilization uses capacity_per_hour.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONGESTION MODEL (EnhancedScheduleOptimizer._calculate_congestion_metrics)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each airport and hour 0–23:
  • dep_count = departures from that airport in that hour (from data).
  • arr_count = arrivals at that airport in that hour (from data).
  • total_count = dep_count + arr_count.
  • utilization = total_count / capacity_per_hour (capped at 1.0 for display column).

Enhanced congestion level (before cap at 1.0):
  base = utilization
  IF hour in airport peak_hours:   base × 1.4
  THEN:                             base × (1 + weather_delays)
  THEN:                             base × ground_congestion

Slot flagging:
  • IsAvoidableSlot = true when utilization > 0.80 (80% of declared hourly capacity).

Interpretation for “efficiency / congestion”:
  • Lower CongestionLevel ⇒ relatively less modeled pressure at that hour.
  • Slots marked avoidable are candidates to shift traffic away from in planning
    discussions; exact operational gain is not a single fixed % — it depends on
    how many movements you move and real airport constraints outside this model.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DELAY RISK PER HOUR (_calculate_delay_patterns, _calculate_enhanced_delay_risk)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per airport and hour (where flights exist in the data):
  • Aggregates mean/median/std of DepDelay and ArrDelay, flight count, and mean
    Cascading_Risk_Score for that hour.

DelayRisk for the hour:
  base_risk = (max(0, mean_dep_delay) + std_dep_delay) / 100
  total_risk = 0.7 × base_risk + 0.3 × mean_cascading_risk_for_hour
  (capped at 1.0)

So “efficiency” at slot level blends empirical delay level/volatility with the
average cascading risk of flights departing in that hour.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. OPTIMAL SLOT RANKING (find_optimal_slots_enhanced)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each hour with both congestion and delay rows present, a composite score is
computed (higher = worse conditions):

  total_score = 0.4 × CongestionLevel
              + 0.3 × DelayRisk
              + 0.3 × CascadingRisk

Hours are sorted by total_score ascending; the lowest scores are the recommended
slots (up to max_slots, default 10).

Qualitative bands (_get_enhanced_recommendation_text / _get_optimality_rating):
  score < 0.25  → EXCELLENT (5 stars)
  score < 0.40  → GOOD (4 stars)
  score < 0.60  → MODERATE (3 stars)
  score < 0.75  → BUSY (2 stars)
  score ≥ 0.75  → AVOID (1 star)

BUSIEST SLOTS LIST: per airport, top 8 hours by CongestionLevel with avoidance
text and suggested nearby lower-congestion alternatives within ±2 hours when
available (CongestionLevel < 0.5).

AVOIDANCE TEXT THRESHOLDS (congestion_level only):
  ≥ 0.9  → AVOID — extreme congestion
  ≥ 0.7  → AVOID — very busy
  ≥ 0.5  → CAUTION — moderate
  <  0.5 → ACCEPTABLE — low congestion


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SCHEDULE TUNER — SIMULATED SHIFT OUTCOMES (ScheduleTuner)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a departure is shifted by N minutes:
  • original_delay_risk / new_delay_risk: fraction of historical flights in the
    dataset with same Origin and DepHour where DepDelay > 15 minutes (after
    updating hour for the hypothetical new time). If no history: default 0.3.
  • risk_change = new − original (negative ⇒ modeled risk reduction at new slot).
  • Cascading: examines up to the next 3 flights on the same aircraft; counts
    affected flights if the shift exceeds available turnaround buffer; sums a
    simple delay propagation estimate in minutes.

This gives a transparent before/after story for “efficiency” of a time change
conditional on your CSV’s historical delays at that airport-hour.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. CASCADING IMPACT ANALYZER (CascadingDelayAnalyzer)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per-flight cascading_impact_score ∈ [0, 1] is a weighted sum:
  • 40%: historical P(DepDelay > 15) at same origin + departure hour.
  • 30%: aircraft utilization intensity (flights per calendar day / 8, capped).
  • 20%: turnaround pressure (<60 min → +0.2; <90 min → +0.15).
  • 10%: peak-hour departure (7–9, 17–20).

Risk categories:
  score ≥ 0.7  HIGH
  score ≥ 0.5  MEDIUM
  score ≥ 0.3  LOW-MEDIUM
  score <  0.3 LOW

YOUR RUN-TIME RESULTS: mean/max impact by origin, and “top N” highest-impact
flights — values come from the dataframe after cleaning (see Flight Ops UI).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. ENHANCED ML MODELS (train_enhanced_models) — DELAY PREDICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Target: ArrDelayBinary = 1 if ArrDelay > 15 minutes.

Engineered features (subset available in data):
  Month, DayOfWeek, DepHour, Distance, AirTime,
  IsPeakHour (per AIRPORT_CONFIG at origin),
  IsWeekend (Sat/Sun),
  HasQuickTurnaround, TurnaroundPressure (<90 min gap),
  Cascading_Risk_Score.

Train/test: 80/20 split, stratified when both classes exist, random_state=42.

Model A — Pipeline: StandardScaler + RandomForestClassifier
  n_estimators=150, max_depth=12, min_samples_split=5, n_jobs=-1

Model B — XGBoost if installed, else GradientBoostingClassifier
  n_estimators=150, max_depth=6, learning_rate=0.1, subsample=0.8
  (XGB: use_label_encoder=False, eval_metric=logloss)

METRICS STORED IN SESSION (meta):
  • rf_acc, model_b_acc — test-set accuracy (floats).
  • model_b_name — "XGBoost" or "GradientBoosting".
  • features — list of feature names actually used.

YOUR RESULTS: Open Flight Operations → tab “ML Model & NLP Query” → expander
“Enhanced Model Performance”; the two accuracy metrics and feature list are the
project outputs for this block. They change every time the underlying dataframe
changes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. PILOT FATIGUE RANDOM FOREST (PilotFatiguePredictor)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Training data: synthetic (n=1000, seed=42). Features are uniform-random within
ranges; labels follow deterministic rules:
  Critical (2):  hours ≥ 9 OR ratio ≥ 2.0 OR workload ≥ 80
  Moderate (1):  else if hours ≥ 6 OR ratio ≥ 1.2 OR workload ≥ 55
  Efficient (0): otherwise

Model: RandomForestClassifier, n_estimators=100, max_depth=5, random_state=42.

Operational mapping from schedule to features (fatigue_features_for_pilot_day /
_fatigue_triple_from_totals): aggregates per pilot per calendar day — total block
time, sector count, delayed leg count — then maps to (avg_flight_hours proxy,
duty_to_rest_ratio proxy, workload_index proxy) with clipping rules in code.

IMPORTANT: This is NOT calibrated on real biometric data; it encodes a consistent
stress proxy for demo/decision support. Do not treat probabilities as regulatory
compliance evidence.

DecisionSupportFramework hard rules (applied alongside ML):
  • Reject if block hours > 10 or duty/rest ratio > 2.5 (deterministic).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. OPTIMIZE SCHEDULE (propose_optimized_schedule) — CREW / SPACING ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Explicitly documented in code: does NOT use airport congestion or delay-based
slot shifting for this step. It uses the fatigue RF only.

Parameters (fixed in code):
  ALT_CRIT_MAX = 0.55     (alternate pilot’s critical class probability must stay ≤ this)
  MIN_CRIT_DROP = 0.03    (minimum drop in original pilot’s critical prob to accept swap)
  MAX_PASSES = 3          (outer refinement passes)
  MAX_ALTS_CAP / MAX_ALTS_FO = min(12, max(1, n−1)) for captain / FO pools

Actions:
  1) Swap Captain or First Officer on the longest leg of a high-risk pilot-day
     when an alternate reduces critical probability enough without pushing the
     alternate over ALT_CRIT_MAX.
  2) Spacing pass: if still critical and no prior change on row, shift STD/STA
     by up to min(max_shift_minutes, 20) minutes later (default max_shift 30).

Displayed “Risk_Reduction_%” is a derived display metric from probability deltas
(bounded in code), not a measured operational KPI.

YOUR RESULTS: rows with Change_Reason and Risk_Reduction_% after clicking
optimize; compare counts and reasons in the exported/optimized dataframe.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. FLIGHT OPS DASHBOARD KPIs (typical on-screen outputs)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
With data loaded, the app commonly shows:
  • Total flights, number of configured airports (6).
  • Average Cascading_Risk_Score across df.
  • Count of flights with Cascading_Risk_Score > 0.7 (“high risk” threshold).

These are instantaneous summaries of the current master/optimized dataframe.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT CONTAIN (honesty clause)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  • No single “project-wide % efficiency gain” — the tool does not run a closed-
    form A/B against a live airline baseline; improvements are local (per slot,
    per simulated shift, per optimized row).
  • No fixed accuracy for fatigue RF on real operations (synthetic labels).
  • No guarantee that congestion multipliers match real NOTAM/capacity notices;
    they are configurable priors in AIRPORT_CONFIG.

To capture run-specific numbers for external review, record from the UI at the
same time as your CSV: ML accuracies, KPI metrics, top cascading flights, optimal
slot tables for chosen airports, and optimize-schedule change summary.
